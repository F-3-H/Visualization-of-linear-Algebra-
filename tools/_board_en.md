### [2026-10-14] When a criterion falls outside "what the readout can recompute", report a different quantity that stays monotonically readable near the degeneracy — do not loosen the threshold and do not drop it

- Hypothesis/Observation: this round's deliverable was a five-view interactive project plus an article (linear algebra basics, control-theory instalment: a stick standing on a palm). One view asks the reader to drag "where the force enters" (a column b) onto either eigen-direction, to see that "one direction is out of reach". The criterion printed in the readout is the area det[b, A·b]; the area collapsing to 0 means out of reach.

- Practice result: **the criterion was right; the number of digits it was printed with was not.** "Out of reach" is **one point at the 1e−9 scale, not a region** (b must land exactly on the eigen-direction; rounded to three decimals it becomes 1e−4 and that cell can no longer be touched) — and a readout can only print finitely many digits. So a very awkward state arose: **the criterion in the readout is correct, yet the reader can never recompute that zero from the readout.** The verification side fell into the same hole first: group C independently recomputed from "the b printed to three decimals" and got 7e−5 instead of 0, which looks like the page is wrong — but it was **the verification method** mistaking rounding for fact. Neither fix loosened the threshold: ① the readout **additionally prints a quantity that stays monotonic near the degeneracy and that the reader can recompute directly from the readout** (this round: the angle between the two columns b and A·b, folded onto 0–90° as "two lines", because when b points along the "returns" line the two columns are opposite and atan2 returns 180°); ② verification was split into two checks each standing on its own — the readout's **self-consistency** (if it says out of reach, it must also give 0 area and 0 angle) and its agreement with the **exact eigen-direction**, rather than with the rounded b.

- Conclusion: **"is the criterion correct" and "is the criterion inside what the readout can recompute" are two independent questions.** Once a criterion is an **equality** on some quantity (det = 0, angle = 0, rank deficiency, a singularity) and that quantity is also shown to the reader, at least one of two things must hold: the printed digits let the reader recompute that equality, or a second quantity that is **still monotonically readable near the equality** carries the information out. The older framing rule says "what the readout can measure, the picture must show"; this one is its other half: **what the picture shows, the readout must let you compute** — the two together are the complete form of "readout and picture share one source". One corollary for the verification side: **when the criterion is an equality, recomputing from the rounded readout necessarily yields "it looks wrong" — that is not a defect found, that is a false positive the method brings with it.**

- Status: Pending hypothesis (one round of practice; both the page side and the verification side were measured and fixed, forward 394/0 and reverse 174 failing assertions with all four groups A/B/C/D catching. The criterion is sharp and reproducible: "for any criterion printed for the reader, ask — from this line of text, can the reader compute that equality themselves?")

- Theory revision suggestion: none. Connects to the existing "evidence ladder ordered by credibility": this fills in the ladder's **bottom rung** — first settle "can the reader recompute from the deliverable itself", then talk about higher-level evidence.
